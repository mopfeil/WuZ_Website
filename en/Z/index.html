Master of Chance

The trick with the symbol chips and the box. You run a chance experiment in which your spectator does all the shaking, and you still know in advance which chip will be left.

Before you read on: magicians do not give away secrets. This page is for you because you want to perform the trick yourself. Keep the secret to yourself, and practise before you show it.

What your audience sees

Seven chips come out of a small box. Each one has a symbol on one side, and the other side is blank. Your spectator checks this for themselves. Then they shake the chips in their cupped hands and let them fall onto the table. Every chip that lands blank side up is out. The rest are shaken again, and again, until only one is left.

It is the circle. And that is exactly what you drew on a slip of paper beforehand.

What you need

  • Eight chips. Seven have a symbol on one side and a blank back: circle, cross, waves, square, star, triangle and heart. The eighth chip has the circle on both sides. That is the double chip, and it is the secret.
  • The box with the groove lid. Seven chips fit in the box in a stack; the eighth sits at the top, inside the lid.
  • A small slip of paper with a circle drawn on it, folded. This is your prediction.

The secret of the lid

Inside the lid, above a narrow rim, there is a chamber that holds exactly one chip. A groove runs all the way round it, a little wider than the opening. When the box is screwed shut, the top chip lies in this chamber.

Hold: tilt the closed box towards you and the top chip slides to the low side, pushing its edge into the groove. If you now take the lid off and keep that side low, the chip comes away with the lid. It hangs at a slight angle in the groove, wedges itself and stays hidden inside the lid.

Release: tilt the lid the other way, with the side where the chip hangs clearly upwards, and the chip slides out of the groove and drops. A light tap helps.

The groove runs all the way round, so you do not need a mark. Just remember: tilted towards you means hold, tilted away from you means release.

Preparation

Put the seven one-sided chips into the box, the double chip on top, and screw the lid on. Put the folded slip with the circle on the table, or give it to someone to look after.

Performance

  1. Open and hold. Tilt the box a little towards you and unscrew the lid. The double chip stays in the lid. Put the lid on the table with its opening facing down.
  2. Show. Pour the seven chips into your spectator's hand. Have them turn over every chip: each has a symbol on one side and nothing on the other.
  3. Release. While they are checking the chips, rest the lid loosely on the empty box so nothing gets lost. As you do so, tilt it away from you: the double chip drops unnoticed to the bottom of the box.
  4. Fill, circle last. Take the lid off again and ask for the chips back. Drop them into the box one at a time, naming the symbols. The circle goes in last. Screw the lid on.
  5. The switch. Announce the chance experiment. Tilt the box towards you again, unscrew the lid and put it aside, opening down. The one-sided circle is now in the lid, and the box holds six one-sided chips and the double chip. That is seven again, just as before.
  6. The experiment. Pour the chips into your spectator's cupped hands. They shake them and let them fall onto the table. Every chip with the blank side up is out; slide them aside, blank side up.
  7. Repeat. Gather the remaining chips without turning them over and hand them back for the next shake. Keep going until only one chip is left on the table. It usually takes three to five rounds.
  8. The reveal. The circle is left. Explain that you are master of chance, because you knew this in advance. Have the slip unfolded.

Why it always works

The double chip always shows a circle, however it lands, so it can never be knocked out. Every other chip goes out with a probability of one half in each round. In the end the double chip is the one that is inevitably left. The check at the start was honest; it just took place with different chips from the experiment.

The lid does the switch. In the original trick from the booklet you hide the double chip in your hand (“palming”) and swap it for the one-sided chip as you tip your hands over. With the box the same thing happens without any sleight of hand.

Tips

  • Practise holding and releasing until you no longer need to look. Try out beforehand how far you have to tilt the lid for the chip to slide out.
  • Never show the inside of the lid to the audience. Put down with its opening facing down, it just looks like a lid.
  • When you fill the box in step 4, keep it out of the light. The double chip is already lying at the bottom.
  • Do not reach into the box to take chips out; always pour them out. And do not let anyone screw the box shut and shake it, or the chip in the lid will slide out.
  • After the performance, put the box in your pocket. To reset, pour out all the chips on your own, put the seven one-sided chips back, the double chip on top, and screw the lid on.

And what does this have to do with science?

Your spectator properly checked that all the chips were alike, and did the shaking themselves. Even so, the result was fixed in advance. A check is only useful if it tests exactly the material the experiment then runs with. In research this means disclosing the experimental conditions, not just checking them at the start, and having results replicated by others. A result that could not have turned out any other way proves nothing, however convincing it looks.
